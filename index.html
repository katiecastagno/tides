Hour by hour

  • Gusts are forecast only at 10 m. The check uses whichever is higher: the 10 m gust or the sustained wind at your altitude, since gusts aloft are usually stronger than at the surface.
  • Ceiling is an estimate: the lowest model level with ≥50% cloud cover (about 350, 1,000, 1,700 and 2,500 ft). It's also flagged as a caution when low cloud is ≥50% and the temperature/dew point spread suggests a low base. Confirm with the nearest airport report ().
  • Thunderstorms (⛈) are NO-GO. High CAPE (energy available for storms) is a CAUTION, since storms can pop up even when the hourly forecast shows little rain.
  • Kp is the planetary geomagnetic index from NOAA SWPC, forecast in 3-hour blocks about 3 days ahead (– beyond that). Kp 5+ is a geomagnetic storm and can degrade GNSS/RTK accuracy. It's a caution, not a no-go.
  • Sun is the sun's elevation for imagery planning and doesn't affect GO/NO-GO. "Long shadows" means low sun. "Glint risk" means high sun reflecting off water and wet sand. "Even light" means overcast (≥80% cloud), which is often best for photogrammetry.
  • Measured wind comes from the tide station's sensor, where it has one (Boston does), and is compared with the 10 m forecast for today.
  • 2,000 ft horizontal clearance from clouds can't be forecast. Check it by eye on site.
  • 🌅 Civil twilight (sun 0–6° below the horizon) is shown separately in pink. Part 107 allows flying then only with anti-collision lighting visible for 3 statute miles. Windows that are daylight show the twilight minutes on either side as extras.
  • Check airspace (LAANC / B4UFLY) and TFRs before every flight. Forecast from Open-Meteo. Not a substitute for a preflight weather briefing.
